# Tesla History and Research Corpus

Purpose: This document is a retrieval corpus for a research agent. Use only retrieved passages from this document when answering factual questions. Every numeric claim must state its fiscal year and source ID.

## Company timeline

- 2003: Tesla Motors was founded. [S1]
- 2008: Tesla began Roadster deliveries, establishing its first production vehicle. [S1, S2]
- 2010: Tesla completed its initial public offering. [S1, S3]
- 2012: Model S launched, moving Tesla toward a premium sedan platform. [S2]
- 2015: Tesla introduced Powerwall, expanding into energy storage. [S2, S4]
- 2016: Tesla acquired SolarCity and continued scaling Gigafactory Nevada. [S2, S4]
- 2017: Model 3 production began. The program was central to Tesla's high-volume strategy and introduced major manufacturing-scale challenges. [S2, S4]
- 2019: Gigafactory Shanghai began production, creating a major manufacturing base in China. [S4, S5]
- 2020: Tesla joined the S&P 500 and reported its first full-year GAAP profit. [S5]
- 2021: Tesla moved its headquarters to Texas and expanded production activity in Austin and Berlin. [S6]
- 2023: Tesla began Cybertruck deliveries. [S8]
- 2024: Tesla described artificial intelligence, autonomy, energy storage, manufacturing efficiency, and its vehicle business as major long-term strategic areas. [S9]

## Interpretation rules

- Revenue rose from 2020 through 2023 and was broadly flat in 2024.
- GAAP net income rose through 2023 and declined in 2024.
- Vehicle deliveries declined from 1,808,581 in 2023 to 1,789,226 in 2024.
- Do not confuse deliveries with production, revenue with gross profit, or GAAP results with non-GAAP results.
- Do not make a number-based claim unless the retrieved evidence supports the exact metric, value, unit, and reporting period.
- This document describes historical reported information. It is not investment advice and does not make a forecast.

## Business model

Tesla reports Automotive, Energy Generation and Storage, and Services and Other revenues. Its operations include vehicle design and manufacturing, charging, energy storage, solar, software, manufacturing automation, and autonomy/AI development.

## Research risks to validate in source filings

An analyst should validate competition, pricing pressure, demand volatility, supply availability, commodity costs, manufacturing ramps, new-product launches, regulatory matters, warranty/service obligations, international operations, energy-product execution, and autonomy/AI execution.
